## 1. The subject

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Subject | SET490, SE Graduation Thesis (Khóa luận tốt nghiệp Kỹ thuật phần mềm), syllabus 14181 |
| Approval | Decision 377/QĐ-ĐHFPT dated 04/09/2026 |
| Credits and hours | 10 credits, 500 study hours: 33.75 contact hours (one slot a week for 15 weeks), 1.5 hours of defence, 464.75 hours of self-study |
| Method | Inquiry-based, experiential, project-based and research-based learning |
| Pass mark | Average 5 of 10; every on-going part needs at least 2 of 10 |
| Language | English in every project document and progress report |

**Product size.** A team of 4 to 5 delivers the equivalent of **8 to 10 medium use cases**, each of 3 to 7
transactions (a screen action button, an end-user interaction or a database transaction counts as one).

**Research component.** The project must include problem definition, literature review, a proposed solution or
model, and experimental evaluation, and must justify technical decisions on practical grounds and on existing
research. Expected artefacts: research proposal and plan, literature review, methodology report with data
collection results, experiments and results, discussion with conclusion and future work, and the software
package (source code, deployment package, installation guide, user manuals) with a paper if any.

**Attendance.** At least 80 % of the scheduled supervisor meetings, or the student cannot defend.

**Learning outcomes.** CLO1 problem identification and research gap; CLO2 literature review and synthesis;
CLO3 research design and methodology; CLO4 proposed solution or model; CLO5 implementation and experimental
evaluation; CLO6 analysis and critical discussion; CLO7 research communication and integration.

## 2. Assessment

| Part | Weight | Content |
|---|---|---|
| On-going assessment (OGA), 7 reports | 50 % | Report 1 Project Introduction 5 %, Report 2 Research Proposal and Plan 5 %, Report 3 Literature Review/Evaluation 15 %, Report 4 Methodology Report and Data Collection Results 25 %, Report 5 Experimental and Results 25 %, Report 6 Discussion, Conclusion and Future Work 15 %, Report 7 Final Project Report 10 % |
| Final thesis presentation | 50 % | About 30 minutes of presentation and 60 minutes of demo and Q&A. Project Introduction 5 %, Research Proposal and Plan 5 %, Literature Review 15 %, Methodology and Data Collection 25 %, Experimental and Results 25 %, Discussion, Conclusion and Future Work 10 %, Presentation skills 5 %, Q&A 10 % |

The presentation mark is the average of the committee members' marks for each student. The research guide adds
a personal mark between 100 % of the group mark and plus or minus 20 % of it, for every report.

**Barred from the defence:** a member with OGA under 5, or found cheating (cannot prove they built the
software themselves).

**Second defence round** when the whole team's OGA is under 5, or in any of these cases: (1) incomplete research
components; (2) insufficient literature review (too few peer-reviewed papers, no critical comparison); (3) no
research contribution (no model, algorithm, framework or meaningful improvement); (4) missing or invalid
experiments (no dataset, no evaluation metrics, improper method); (5) poor implementation quality (incomplete,
non-functional, or not the proposed solution); (6) weak analysis and discussion; (7) required documents not
submitted; (8) no traceability between the research and the system; (9) academic integrity, similarity above
30 %, plagiarism, falsified data or uncited sources; (10) inadequate presentation readiness. The project is
redone when these cases recur at the second defence or any OGA part is under 2.

**Failed after the defence**, whatever the supervisor's evaluation: (1) a student cannot demonstrate any
function or use case; (2) cannot prove they prepared any report or the software; (3) the submitted software has
more than 3 obvious coding-logic defects or more than 1 show-stopper.

## 4. The report templates

Every template opens with Abbreviations and a Record of Changes table (Date, A/M/D, In charge, Change
Description). The section skeletons:

| Report | Sections |
|---|---|
| 1 Project Introduction | Overview (project information, team); Product Background; Problem Statement; Research Objectives (general, specific); Scope and Limitations of Study; Significance of the Study |
| 2 Research Proposal and Plan | Research direction and conceptual framework (WBS, opportunity identification); Management approach (gap positioning, proposed research concept and solution vision as input, process, output, strategic research approach with alternatives compared); Research feasibility and resources (technical, data, hardware and skills); Expected research outcomes (outputs, validation methods, success indicators); Responsibility assignments; Research roadmap and milestones; Configuration management (dataset, source code) |
| 3 Literature Review | Introduction; Conceptual foundations and core theories; Problem space and domain challenges; Existing research (technology landscape, approaches in previous research, evaluation metrics from previous work); Fit-gap analysis; Summary |
| 4 Methodology | Research design or proposed framework; Data collection methods; Sampling and data analysis techniques (tools named and justified); Proposed algorithms or system implementation details; Evaluation strategy; Limitations of the methodology (data, scalability, hardware, scope, time, ethics, generalisability) |
| 5 Experimental and Result | Introduction; Presentation of data; Analysis of results; Interpretation; Comparison with existing research results; Implications. The template points to `Example_report2.pdf` and `Example_paper.pdf` |
| 6 Discussion and Future Work | Discussion of key findings (performance, effectiveness, robustness and generalisation); Theoretical and practical contributions; Limitations of the study (data, method, technical, time and resources); Challenges and open problems; Future work; Report summary |
| 7 Final Report | Reports 1 to 6 merged: introduction, problem background with WBS, feasibility, outcomes, roles, roadmap, configuration management (with tools and infrastructure); literature review; methodology; experiments and results; discussion and future work |

The research guide asks for a thesis with title page, a self-contained abstract (no abbreviations, references or
URLs), 3 to 10 keywords, introduction, literature review, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion and
future work, IEEE-style references numbered in order of appearance, and appendices.
