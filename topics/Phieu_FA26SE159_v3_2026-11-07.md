**(\*) 3.2. Main proposal content (including result and product)**

**a) Context:**

==**The problem.** Trekking companies lead groups along mountain routes where mobile phone coverage is weak or missing for long stretches. Satellite messengers need both the device and a paid satellite subscription [14], so they cost more to buy and run, and few groups carry them. In Vietnam, the market for trekking and off-grid communication systems is still new and has room to grow.==

==**The solution.** TrekLink builds LoRa mesh devices and rents them by plan to organizations through a single platform that TrekLink hosts. An SOS alerts the organization's own on-duty staff first and goes to TrekLink Staff when nobody answers. The team's Meshtastic-based firmware [6] relays messages over several hops, and the platform adds live monitoring.==

In Summer 2026 the project team developed TrekLink, an open-source LoRa mesh communication firmware for ESP32/ESP32-S3 devices. The team wrote the firmware on top of Meshtastic, and it supports multi-hop mesh messaging, GPS tracking, fall detection and automatic SOS broadcasting. The firmware is kept under version control and is the technical foundation that this Capstone inherits. The Capstone does not rebuild it.

==TrekLink is a smart-device management platform whose customers are trekking companies. It does not manage tours. TrekLink owns the devices, the firmware, the Field Station software and the cloud platform, and rents the devices to organizations by plan. Many organizations share the one platform. Each of them manages its own members, on-duty roster, rented devices, alerts and API keys on it, and keeps its trips, customers and emergency response outside it.==

==**Scope disclaimer.** TrekLink is a supplementary safety and coordination tool with no guaranteed rescue link. A device works only within LoRa range of another TrekLink node, a relay checkpoint or the Field Station. The Field Station reaches the cloud only while it has an internet connection. The device fixes its position by GPS and shares it over LoRa only, with no satellite uplink. A device out of range keeps beaconing, and its own durable queue delivers the stored events once a node hears it. When the battery runs out, the organization receives a stale-device alert with the last known position and trail, and no further data. When no node hears an SOS, the device repeats it until one does, and the platform flags a suspected episode from the beacon pattern. Range drops sharply in heavy rain, behind a ridge with no relay on the crest and through thick concrete.==

**Scope Boundary:** ==The LoRa mesh firmware and the hardware that this team built in Summer 2026 are the technical foundation. Targeted firmware enhancements made during the Capstone, such as the durable on-device queue, the SOS beaconing fixes and radio power compliance, are in scope and credited to the Capstone. Rearchitecting the mesh stack is out of scope. The Capstone covers the Field Station gateway bridge, offline-resilient synchronization, the cloud backend, organization and asset management, rental contracts, incident management with tiered alerts, live telemetry with an organization API, billing, system integration and experimental evaluation.==

==The Capstone serves the following needs:==

- ==**Trekkers and guides** need to share their position, exchange preset messages and raise an alert from the device itself, without a phone, in places a phone cannot reach.==

- ==**Trekking companies** need to see their own groups and devices and to receive alerts that have a clear owner and a record, without buying or maintaining the hardware.==

- ==**Local authorities and rescue units** need a timely report with the last known position and the signal history when an incident escalates.==

- ==**TrekLink, as the provider,** needs to track the custody, condition and charges of each rented device across many renters, and to deliver field events through long outages.==

**b) Proposed Solutions:**

TrekLink extends the existing LoRa mesh firmware into ==an enterprise device-rental platform built on asset management. The platform has six== core capabilities:

- **==Organization and rental management:==** ==Organizations register themselves, and TrekLink verifies and approves them. They rent devices on a monthly plan or a day plan, with a minimum order quantity. On the monthly plan, half the term fee is paid at the start as a holding fee and half at the end, and the contract renews until the organization gives notice. A day plan lasts 3, 4 or 7 days, is paid in full at handover and costs more per day than the monthly rate. Handover and return take place in person at the TrekLink counter with a signed handover note. Payments are recorded online through a third-party payment gateway, VietQR in its sandbox, or at the counter. Each organization sorts the devices it holds under all its contracts into its own groups with one level of subgroups, for example one group per site with smaller teams inside it, and labels each device with the name of its holder. TrekLink does not model trips or tours, so each organization uses groups for its own purpose. The organization's staff can also place a rented node on site as a temporary relay checkpoint and retrieve it later. Relays belong to the same contract, and a node left on site after the contract ends counts as lost.==

The contract renews automatically, the organization can give notice during a running term, and the contract finally closes after return and inspection. A day-plan contract is paid in full at handover and has a due date. A late fee is charged per day after the grace period, and the contract closes after return and inspection.==

- **==Asset management:==** ==Each physical device carries two identities, a printed asset tag and its radio node number, and moves through a nine-state lifecycle: In Intake, Available, Reserved, Rented, Returned, Maintenance, Lost, Retired and Disposed. On the main path, an Available device becomes Reserved when a contract is approved, Rented at handover and Returned at check-in. A Returned device goes back to Available after a reset and a passed inspection, or to Maintenance after a failed one. A device in Maintenance becomes Available after repair or is Retired, and a Retired device becomes Disposed after a verified wipe. A loss can be reported from any of the six working states. It opens a loss case, which ends either with the device recovered to Returned or with the loss settled and the device Retired. The renter bears a loss only when the device was in its custody. At handover each device is provisioned with the renter's channel key, and on return it is reset and inspected before it can be rented again.==

- **Offline-Resilient ==Field Station==:** Each field event is ==identified by eventId = sha256(nodeNum : packetId), which is derived from identifiers the firmware already transmits. The Field Station is a single executable that the organization runs on its own laptop, with one rented node on a USB port, at a fixed base point of its choice such as a basecamp, a vehicle, a ranger station or a trailhead. It uploads over whatever internet connection it finds there, site Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot.== When the internet is unavailable, the ==Field Station== stores events persistently in a local SQLite queue ==and shows the local mesh to the operators on site==. SOS events are safety-critical and get priority P0, so they are synchronized first on reconnection. GPS updates are P2 and routine telemetry is P3. ==Each device also keeps its own durable queue. An event records when it occurred and the source of that time, and it belongs to the organization that held the device at that moment. An event that synchronizes after the device was returned and rented again therefore reaches the earlier renter and never the new one.==

- **Idempotent Event Processing:** The cloud backend uses the Event ID as an idempotency key [9]. The same event may arrive several times through retransmission, replay after reconnection or a duplicate LoRa broadcast, and the backend still creates exactly one Incident ==and one alert per tier==. This prevents alert fatigue and keeps the data consistent.

- **Incident Management ==with tiered alerts==:** ==Each SOS episode becomes one incident, routed to the organization that holds the device. Alerts go to the organization's on-duty member, then to the backup and then to the Manager, each after a timeout. If nobody acknowledges, the incident escalates to TrekLink, which reports it to the authorities. The organization runs the response and reports its status back. Each transition is atomic and is audited with the actor, the time and the reason.==

- **==Live telemetry:==** ==Each organization sees only its own rented devices, positions, battery levels and alerts on a live map. It can read the same data in its own systems through a REST and WebSocket API with an organization key.==

**d) Non-Functional Requirements:**

- **API Performance:** At least 95% of normal backend API requests shall complete within 300 ms under the defined test workload of 50 concurrent users.

- **Synchronization Latency:** When gateway uplink connectivity is available, newly received field events shall reach the cloud backend within 5 seconds under normal load.

- **Offline Recovery: Delivery Rate:** The Gateway shall achieve a message delivery rate of ≥ 99% after connectivity recovery under the defined experimental conditions: connectivity-loss durations of 30 s to 30 min and at least 20 trials per condition.

- **Offline Recovery: Testability:** Connectivity loss shall be reproducible in a controlled lab environment by programmatically interrupting the gateway's network interface, so that experiments can be repeated.

- **Duplicate Prevention:** Repeated delivery of an SOS event with the same eventId shall create zero duplicate Incident records. A dedicated automated test shall verify this by delivering the same SOS event 10 times and asserting that exactly one Incident is created.

- **Priority Ordering:** On each reconnection, all P0 (SOS) events queued during the offline period shall be delivered to the backend before any P2 or P3 events. The priority-ordering compliance rate shall be ≥ 99% across all experimental trials.

- **Concurrency:** The backend shall process at least 20 simultaneous gateway event submissions without message loss or duplicate Incident creation, verified by a concurrent load test.

- **Offline Resilience:** Loss of cloud connectivity shall not interrupt local LoRa mesh communication or device-level SOS propagation on the field devices.

- **Data Consistency:** Replayed gateway messages shall not create duplicate incidents or duplicate telemetry records. Idempotency keyed on eventId at the backend enforces this.

- **Security:** JWT-based authentication, RBAC, TLS-encrypted transport, bcrypt password hashing and auditable access to sensitive ==incident data==. ==No organization shall be able to read another organization's devices, positions, incidents, contracts or invoices, and a cross-organization test per module shall verify this.==

- **Auditability:** Each incident state transition, device lifecycle transition and ==rental contract== event shall be traceable to an actor, identified by user ID and role, and to a UTC timestamp.

- **Scalability:** The system shall support at least ==10 organizations and 50 rented TrekLink devices concurrently== without architectural changes.

- **Deployability:** All server components shall support containerized deployment with Docker Compose and automated CI/CD with GitHub Actions.

**e) Theory & Practical:**

**Theory: The project applies software engineering and distributed systems principles to integrate an intermittently connected LoRa mesh network with a cloud-based operational platform.**

- **Finite State Machines:** Model ==the nine-state device lifecycle with its loss case, the incident lifecycle with tiered notification and escalation, and the organization and rental-contract lifecycles== as state machines with controlled, auditable transitions [8]. Each transition is an atomic, logged operation.

- **Distributed Systems and Eventual Consistency:** MQTT loosely couples the Gateway and the cloud backend. Field events are produced offline and delivered when connectivity allows, and the backend converges to a consistent state once all queued events are processed [7].

- **Priority-Aware Store-and-Forward:** Classify field events by safety criticality into P0 SOS, P1 incident-related location, P2 GPS tracking and P3 routine telemetry, and keep that order during the queue flush so that safety-critical events are not delayed behind routine data [5].

- **Idempotent Event Processing:** Backend event handlers produce the same outcome however many times an event is delivered, with eventId as a natural idempotency key [9]. Reliability is therefore handled above the transport layer, without relying on exactly-once delivery from MQTT.

- **IoT Messaging Protocols:** MQTT QoS 1 delivers each message at least once [4]. Combined with idempotency in the backend, it gives effectively exactly-once processing without QoS 2.

- **Event-Driven Architecture:** An SOS event starts an asynchronous pipeline of event ingestion, idempotency check, incident creation, role-based notification and audit logging. The stages are decoupled from each other and can be tested independently.

- **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) ==and multi-tenancy==:** The API layer enforces fine-grained permissions for ==TrekLink Admin and Staff and for each organization's Manager and Operators, and the server isolates each organization's data==.

- **Software Architecture:** A domain-driven modular design separates ==gateway synchronization, organizations, devices, rental contracts, incidents, monitoring, billing and platform services== into independent NestJS modules with documented REST and WebSocket APIs.

**3.3. Research Information**

**a. Research Problem / Research Question**

==Trekking companies and similar organizations that operate where cellular coverage is limited need field events, including SOS alerts, to reach their staff even after a long outage. They also need each SOS to be handled as a coordinated incident with an owner, a tracked acknowledgment and an audit trail.==

==The Summer 2026 TrekLink firmware provides the field communication layer. This Capstone adds a Field Station with priority-aware offline queuing and idempotent event processing, and an SOS-to-Incident pipeline with a formally defined lifecycle and tiered escalation. It then evaluates both.==

The evaluation answers three research questions:

- **RQ1:** How reliably can the TrekLink Gateway Bridge synchronize SOS, GPS, and telemetry events from an intermittently connected LoRa mesh to the cloud backend, measured by message delivery rate, data-loss rate, and duplicate rate?

- **RQ2:** How do varying connectivity-loss durations and reconnection patterns affect message delivery rate, synchronization latency, and priority-ordering compliance of the proposed Gateway architecture?

- **RQ3:** Compared with the existing uncoordinated device-level SOS workflow, to what extent does the TrekLink SOS-to-Incident pipeline improve incident response coordination, traceability, and acknowledgment consistency, as measured by MTTA, MTTR, traceability score, and incident-completion rate in controlled emergency drills?

RQ1 and RQ2 evaluate the Event Reliability & Offline Synchronization functional requirements. RQ3 evaluates the Incident Management functional requirements and the ==incident FSM==. The three RQs trace back to the functional requirements, system modules, task packages and experiment conditions defined in this proposal.

**Baseline definition for RQ3:** In the existing workflow, a TrekLink device triggers an SOS broadcast across the LoRa mesh. If a guide's Meshtastic-paired phone receives the alert, the guide contacts staff by voice call or a messaging application. The workflow has no structured acknowledgment, timestamp recording, response-action log or audit trail. Baseline MTTA is the time from the SOS trigger on the device to the first documented verbal or text acknowledgment, recorded by an independent observer. The baseline traceability score is 0% by definition, because no audit record exists. The study does not assume in advance that MTTA will fall, and the outcomes will be reported as measured.

**b. Research Objectives**

- Design and implement a priority-aware, offline-resilient Gateway Bridge with end-to-end idempotent event processing that connects the TrekLink LoRa mesh to a cloud backend.

- Evaluate gateway synchronization reliability under a controlled matrix of connectivity-loss durations and reconnection patterns, using physical TrekLink hardware for all mesh-to-gateway measurements.

- Design and implement an SOS-to-Incident pipeline with a formally defined ==FSM, tiered notification and escalation==, and an append-only audit trail.

- Evaluate the efficiency of the incident-response workflow by MTTA, MTTR, traceability score and incident-completion rate in randomized controlled emergency drills, against the uncoordinated baseline.

- Integrate the evaluated gateway and incident mechanisms into a complete ==multi-organization rental platform covering asset management, rental contracts, live telemetry and billing==.

**c. Research Scope & Methodology**

In scope: TrekLink ESP32/ESP32-S3 devices and the LoRa mesh running the ==Summer 2026 firmware with targeted enhancements==; the ==Field Station== with LoRa-to-MQTT-to-cloud synchronization, a priority queue and idempotency; the SOS, GPS and selected telemetry message types; connectivity-loss and recovery experiments in a controlled lab environment; evaluation of the SOS-to-Incident workflow in controlled drills; ==the organization, asset and rental-contract modules with multi-tenancy==; a responsive web client for desktop and tablet; ==payment in the sandbox only==.

Out of scope: ==mesh-stack rearchitecture==, native mobile apps, commercial payment, hardware radio certification==, individual renters, and the organizations' own trip and rescue operations==.

Methodology:

- **Literature Review:** Survey LoRa mesh systems [1], store-and-forward and DTN protocols [5], IoT fleet management and emergency incident workflows [3], and identify the integration gap between them.

- **Architecture & Design:** Freeze the firmware message schema and eventId structure. Design the gateway synchronization architecture, SQLite priority queue schema, idempotency table, incident FSM and experiment protocol. Produce the SRS, UML state machine diagrams, architecture diagrams and sequence diagrams.

- **Implementation:** Develop the gateway bridge, backend modules and web client according to TP1 to TP5.

- **Experiment 1: Gateway Synchronization (RQ1 & RQ2):** Use 3–5 physical TrekLink devices for all mesh-to-gateway reliability measurements. MQTT simulation scripts may add Gateway-to-Cloud workload only and are not used to claim LoRa RF reliability. Apply a connectivity-loss matrix of 0 s as the control, 30 s, 2 min, 5 min, 10 min and 30 min, with two reconnection patterns, immediate and delayed by 60 s. Run at least 20 trials per condition. Measures: delivery rate (%), data-loss rate (%), duplicate rate (%), sync latency (mean / median / P95 in seconds), queue-recovery time (s) and priority-ordering compliance rate (%).

- **Experiment 2: SOS-to-Incident Workflow (RQ3):** Run controlled emergency drills with ==two organizations at once, each with 1 TrekLink device, 1 Field Station, an on-duty member and a backup==, and at least ==4== participants in rotation. The scenario order is randomized. Participants do not know the exact SOS trigger time. An independent observer triggers the SOS and starts an external timer. The backend records all timestamps in the TrekLink condition automatically, and the observer records the baseline timestamps with a hardware stopwatch. Run 15–20 drills per condition and vary the network latency between LAN and a 4G hotspot. Measures: MTTA (mean / median / P95 in seconds), MTTR (mean / median in minutes), traceability score (% of FSM transitions with complete actor, timestamp and action), missed-alert rate (%), duplicate-alert rate (%), incident-completion rate (%)==, and cross-organization leakage, meaning alerts or positions delivered to the wrong organization, expected to be 0==.

- **Integration & System Testing:** End-to-end test cases for all functional requirements; idempotency tests in which 10 deliveries of one eventId create 1 Incident; concurrency tests in which 20 simultaneous events create 0 duplicates; RBAC validation; an audit-log completeness check==; a cross-organization isolation suite with at least two organizations per module, including an event that synchronizes after its device was returned and rented to another organization; and a load test with simulated devices and Field Stations at 10 organizations, 50 rented devices and 20 simultaneous Field Station submissions==.

**e. Related Works / Literature Review (Preliminary)**

- **LoRa mesh communication** (Meshtastic, RAK WisBlock Mesh) [1], [2], [6]: multi-hop messaging, GPS, local SOS broadcast and fall detection. ==These systems are designed to work without any infrastructure. TrekLink builds on them and adds the organization-side layer of device rental and fleet management, store-and-forward to the cloud and an incident pipeline.==

- **IoT fleet management platforms** (AWS IoT Core, Azure IoT Hub, Balena) [12]: device registration, telemetry ingestion, remote monitoring and lifecycle states. Gap: they are generic cloud platforms with no LoRa mesh integration, no store-and-forward for intermittent connectivity and no rental domain.

- **Delay/Disruption-Tolerant and store-and-forward systems** (DTN RFC 4838 Bundle Protocol, MQTT-SN, LoRaWAN ADR) [5], [10], [11]: message persistence and forwarding under intermittent connectivity. Gap: they are protocol-level designs and are not integrated with business workflows or incident management.

- **Incident management systems** (JIRA Service Management, PagerDuty, Opsgenie) [13]: ticket lifecycle, escalation, notification, SLA tracking and audit trail. Gap: they are designed for IT and DevOps, with no IoT field-event ingestion and no outdoor or trekking domain context.

==Gap statement: TrekLink combines five dimensions in one domain-specific platform: off-grid LoRa mesh communication, store-and-forward synchronization under intermittent connectivity, device rental lifecycle management, real-time field monitoring, and structured SOS-to-incident response.==
